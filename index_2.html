Foundations

Let's learn what income investing really is.

Follow the path. Each stop builds on the one before it — by the end you'll know exactly what a covered call ETF is, and why two funds that look different can secretly be the same bet.

01

What's a stock?

A tiny ownership slice of a real company. Buy one share of a coffee chain and you technically own a sliver of every espresso machine and lease. When the company does well, your slice is worth more — and sometimes it pays you a piece of the profits directly. That payment is a dividend.

02

What's an ETF?

A single ticket that holds a basket of stocks (or bonds, or other assets) for you. Instead of researching and buying 50 companies yourself, you buy one fund and instantly own a slice of all 50. It trades on the stock exchange just like a single stock does.

03

What's a covered call ETF?

An ETF that holds real stocks, then also sells "call options" against them — contracts that give someone else the right to buy those stocks at a set price later. In exchange, the fund collects cash today. That cash is where the extra monthly income comes from.

04

Covered calls trade upside for cash

The trade-off: if the stock rallies hard past that set price, the fund's gains get capped — someone else gets to buy in at the old price. Steadier cash flow now, in exchange for giving up some of the biggest gains later.

05

Yield isn't return

A 12% "yield" tells you the distribution rate — not whether the fund is growing, shrinking, or quietly handing you back your own capital. Always check total return (price change + distributions) alongside yield, never instead of it.

06

Return of capital isn't always bad — but check

When a fund distributes more than it earns, the difference often comes back as "return of capital." Sometimes that's tax-efficient timing. Sometimes it's the fund eroding its own value to sustain a payout it can't really afford. The difference shows up in the unit price over time, not the yield.

07

"Diversified" often isn't

Own a Canadian bank covered call ETF and a broad Canadian dividend ETF? You may hold the same six banks twice, just wrapped differently. Overlap hides inside fund names — it only shows up when you compare actual holdings, which is exactly what the tool below does.

08

Fees compound against you, quietly

Covered call and enhanced-yield ETFs often carry 0.4–0.9% higher fees than plain index funds — and "fund of funds" structures can stack fees on top of fees. Small percentages, multiplied by decades, are not small.
