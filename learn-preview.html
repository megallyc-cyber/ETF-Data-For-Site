Seven stops, each building on the last.

↓
01

What's a stock?

A tiny ownership slice of a real company. Buy one share of a coffee chain and you own a sliver of every espresso machine it holds.
Why it matters When the company does well your slice is worth more — and sometimes it pays you part of the profits directly. That payment is a dividend.
02

What's an ETF?

A single ticket that holds a basket of stocks for you. Instead of buying fifty companies yourself, you buy one fund and own a slice of all fifty.
Why it matters It trades on the exchange exactly like a single stock — one price, buy or sell any time the market is open.
03

What's a covered call ETF?

An ETF that holds real stocks, then sells something extra against them to collect cash today. That cash is the monthly income.
The key word "Covered" means the fund already owns the shares it's promising. It isn't betting money it doesn't have.
04

So what's a call option?

A ticket someone buys from the fund. It gives them the right to buy a stock at a set price before a deadline — say $22, when it trades at $20 today.
Where the income comes from They pay for that ticket whether they use it or not. If the stock never reaches $22 it expires worthless and the fund keeps the cash. That cash is the premium.
05

Covered calls trade upside for cash

If the stock rallies hard past that set price, the fund's gains stop there — someone else buys in at the old price.
The bargain Steadier cash now, in exchange for the biggest gains later. Neither good nor bad on its own — it depends what you need.
06

Yield isn't return

A high yield tells you the distribution rate — not whether the fund is growing, shrinking, or handing back your own money.
Read them together Always read total return next to yield, never instead of it. Some funds on this site pay over 80% a year while losing most of their value.
07

What the premium is worth to you

The premium is paid on every share you hold, so the income scales with the size of your position — $0.35 a share on 500 shares is $175 that month.
Careful here Premiums move with volatility, so one good month is not a yearly rate. That is exactly how a fund ends up advertising a number it cannot repeat.
